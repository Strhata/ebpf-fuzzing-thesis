\chapter{Experimental Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter presents the experiments in the order they were run, because each
run's failure motivated the next design change: SFT-1 produced valid but trivial
programs; RL run~1 starved its own gradient; a reward and format redesign followed;
SFT-2 produced deep programs but revealed a diversity weakness; RL run~2 cleared the
starvation but ran into that weakness as a wall. The chapter closes by
characterizing the wall directly.

A note on what counts. Per Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}, the metric of interest is
the number of \emph{unique KCOV PCs reached by valid programs}; verifier pass-rate
is a diagnostic, never a result. A model that emits a single trivial valid program
scores a near-perfect pass-rate and explores nothing. Percentages therefore appear
below only as diagnostics, and always with that caveat.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{SFT-1: Valid but Trivial}
\label{sec:results-sft}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:sft-training}

Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B~\cite{qwen25coder} was fine-tuned on the 27{,}514-example SFT
dataset using rank-16 QLoRA on the Q, K, V, and O projections of all attention
layers, for 3 epochs (9{,}288 gradient steps), effective batch size~8 via gradient
accumulation, maximum sequence length 768~tokens, learning rate
$2\times10^{-4}$ with AdamW 8-bit, BF16 with FlashAttention-2. The evaluation loss
fell from 0.637 to 0.557 and stabilised from the end of epoch~2 onward, with train
and eval loss tracking closely (no significant overfitting).

\subsection{What the Model Could Do}
\label{sec:sft1-capability}

The fine-tuned model learned BPF syntax and register conventions: it generates
programs that the verifier accepts, where the zero-shot base model produces almost
none. But the programs are \emph{trivial} --- typically one or two instructions.
The cause is the representation: the verifier-log assembly format
(Section~\ref{sec:format-decision}) costs roughly 232~tokens per instruction once
the register-state annotations are included, so the completion budget leaves room
for only a handful of instructions. The model could produce valid BPF, but not
programs long enough to exercise anything interesting in the verifier.

As a diagnostic only: measured through the pure-Python encoder, SFT-1's syntactic
accept rate is about 73\% (the frequently-quoted ``60\%'' was an artifact of a
clang parser that mis-reads verifier register-state lines as instructions). Every
accept-rate and coverage figure in this thesis is taken through the pure-Python
encoder path; the clang parser that produced the inflated rejection is used
nowhere in the measurement pipeline. This number is not a result. A 73\% accept rate on
two-instruction programs discovers nothing, and is recorded only to make the point
that \emph{validity is not coverage}. SFT-1 answers RQ1 in the narrow,
prerequisite sense of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}: the model acquired BPF syntax,
not the ability to explore.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{RL Run 1: Gradient Starvation}
\label{sec:results-rl}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

RL run~1 used GRPO with $G=4$, $\beta=0.01$, maximum
completion length 600~tokens, on the local RTX~4070 Laptop GPU, for 8{,}370 steps.
It did not learn. Cumulative new-PC growth ceased at approximately step~1{,}300 and
nothing further was discovered in the remaining $\approx$7{,}000 steps
(Figure~\ref{fig:rl-coverage}, left).

\subsection{Coverage}
\label{sec:coverage}

Two distinct quantities must be separated. The \emph{total} coverage frontier at
the end of the run was 1{,}638 distinct PCs, but this includes the
$\approx$1{,}501 PCs already reached during SFT evaluation; the \emph{incrementally
new} contribution of RL was only 137 PCs. Growth was bursty and brief: no new PCs for the first 97
batches, then a series of bursts up to batch~1{,}241, after which only two further
PCs were ever found (batches 3{,}036 and 4{,}873).

\subsection{Diagnosis: \texttt{reward\_std} Collapse}
\label{sec:plateau-results}

The cause is visible directly in the reward variance
(Figure~\ref{fig:rl-std}, left). Across the run, the within-group reward standard
deviation $\sigma_r$ was zero in 99.5\% of batches (mean $\sigma_r \approx 0.001$).
The GRPO advantage is $A_i=(r_i-\bar r)/\sigma_r$
(Equation~\ref{eq:grpo-advantage}); when $\sigma_r=0$ the gradient is identically
zero and no update occurs --- the gradient-starvation failure mode
of~\cite{grpo-starvation}. The few non-zero-variance batches coincide almost
exactly with the discovery events: a batch that finds a new PC briefly creates
reward spread, then variance returns to zero.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/rl_reward_std.pdf}
  \caption{Within-group reward standard deviation per training step. \textbf{Left
  (RL-1):} $\sigma_r$ is exactly zero in 99.5\% of batches (mean $0.001$) --- the
  gradient starves. \textbf{Right (RL-2 phase~B):} the validity-gated soft-floor
  reward keeps $\sigma_r$ alive (mean $0.224$, range $0.01$--$0.44$), never
  sustained-zero. Source: RL-1 from WandB run \texttt{hapj9sah}; RL-2 from the
  exported step history \texttt{results/rl2\_phaseB\_history.csv} (WandB run
  \texttt{bz5ymfzl}, \texttt{reward/std} column).}
  \label{fig:rl-std}
\end{figure}

The cause was the reward's coarse top tier under near-universal acceptance. SFT-1
was prompted with a \texttt{Status:~VALID} header, and during RL inference it
produced accepted programs 99.6\% of the time; once the coverage frontier saturated
at step~$\approx$1{,}300, essentially every completion was an accepted program with
no new PC, which the v1 reward scores at a flat 0.1, so every completion in a group
received the same value. A second defect --- \texttt{kcov\_validator} returned an
empty PC list for every REJECTED program --- was real but immaterial here: at a
0.3\% rejection rate it touched too few completions to move the batch variance, and
it becomes load-bearing only in the post-redesign regime where rejection is common
(Section~\ref{sec:reward-validation}). The prompt-anchored acceptance, not the KCOV
bug, is the operative cause of run~1's zero variance. The \emph{result} of run~1 is
this diagnosis --- not the 137 PCs it happened to touch.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Reward and Format Redesign}
\label{sec:reward-validation}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two changes followed from the run-1 diagnosis. The format was stripped of its
verbose register-state header, so that real programs --- not just one or two
instructions --- fit the completion budget. And the reward was redesigned to
guarantee within-group variance (Section~\ref{sec:reward-redesign}): first a
verdict-blind depth reward, which was rejected because it optimizes coverage from
programs the verifier never accepts, and then the validity-gated soft-floor reward
(Equation~\ref{eq:reward-v2}) carried into run~2.

A 100-step comparison run validated the two structural fixes. Removing the
\texttt{Status:~VALID} header shifted the verdict distribution from 99.6\% accepted
(run~1) to 11.5\% accepted / 63.2\% rejected / 25.2\% encode-fail, confirming that
the prompt had anchored generation toward valid programs. Crucially, those rejected
programs now returned \emph{non-empty} KCOV traces from the fixed binary, so the
reward function could assign them differentiated depth scores --- the precondition
for non-zero $\sigma_r$ in a predominantly-rejected regime.

\section{SFT-2: Deep Programs, Narrow Distribution}
\label{sec:results-sft2}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

SFT-2 retrains on the stripped format with
\texttt{[coverage]}/\texttt{[novelty]} control tokens, for one epoch
(2{,}408~steps). The capability gain over SFT-1 is real: the model now generates
\emph{deep} programs --- a median of 30 instructions at a 512-token budget, against
SFT-1's one or two (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}). These are programs long enough to
exercise the verifier's analysis.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/depth_collapse.pdf}
  \caption{SFT-2 program-length distribution (canonical bytecode count). The
  distribution is narrow and set by the token budget: mean $30.2\pm3.5$
  instructions at 512~tokens, $57.6\pm8.6$ at 1024 (CV~$\approx0.12$--$0.15$).
  Generations almost never emit a terminating \texttt{exit}, so each runs to the
  token cap; the median is the budget divided by the per-instruction token cost,
  and doubling the budget doubles it. The pinned length is therefore a property of
  how generation terminates, not on its own evidence that the model cannot vary
  length.}
  \label{fig:depth}
\end{figure}

The same figure exposes a weakness, but a weaker one than it first appears. The
length distribution is \emph{narrow} (coefficient of variation $\approx 0.12$ at
512~tokens), yet most of that narrowness is mechanical: the model almost never emits
a terminating \texttt{exit}, so generation runs to the token cap and the length is
fixed by the budget rather than chosen by the model. Doubling the budget shifts the
median from 30 to 58 instructions without widening the spread, exactly as a
budget-pinned length would. Length variety is therefore a weak diversity probe here
and is not on its own evidence of mode collapse; the load-bearing evidence is path
overlap, established in Section~\ref{sec:diversity-wall}. SFT-2's inference accept
rate is low ($\approx 7.5\%$,
a diagnostic, not a result): deep programs have far more surface on which to fail
the verifier than two-instruction ones, so depth and validity trade off. This is
the model that the diversity experiment and RL run~2 are built on.

\section{RL Run 2: Starvation Cleared, Ceiling Intact}
\label{sec:run2}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

RL run~2 trains SFT-2 with the validity-gated soft-floor reward
(Equation~\ref{eq:reward-v2}), $G=16$, in two phases: a 20-step smoke test
(phase~A) and a 200-step run with global novelty enabled (phase~B).

The mechanism result is robust. The reward variance stays alive throughout:
$\sigma_r$ has mean $0.224$ (range $0.01$--$0.44$) over all 207 steps, against
run~1's mean $0.001$ with 99\% of batches at exactly zero
(Figure~\ref{fig:rl-std}). The soft floor structurally guarantees within-group
variance even at the $\approx7\%$ accept rate, so the gradient starvation that
halted run~1 \emph{does not recur}. This is a property of the reward design, not a
trend that could reverse. Run~2 differs from run~1 in several hyperparameters at
once ($G$, the prompt format, the SFT base, $\beta$), so the absence of starvation
is not attributed to the empirical run-to-run comparison; it is attributed to the
structural argument of Section~\ref{sec:reward-redesign}, which keeps $\sigma_r>0$
across the group compositions the policy produces with non-negligible probability.
The single confounded comparison is consistent with that argument, not a substitute
for it.

The coverage result is negative but inconclusive. Cumulative PCs climbed during
training (4{,}320~$\to$~4{,}836 total PCs, including those from rejected programs
via the soft floor) but with a clearly decelerating rate
(Figure~\ref{fig:rl-coverage}, right). On the metric that counts --- unique PCs
from \emph{valid} programs --- run~2's checkpoint scores $\approx$3{,}685 (replicate
range 3{,}606--3{,}833), squarely within the SFT-2 band
(Section~\ref{sec:diversity-wall}): RL did not push coverage beyond the SFT
ceiling. The accept rate measured $\approx7.5\%$ before and $\approx8.7\%$ after,
but on a single under-powered measurement that gap is within run-to-run noise, so
no directional claim is made about it.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/rl_coverage.pdf}
  \caption{Cumulative unique PCs per training step (total, including
  rejected-program PCs). \textbf{Left (RL-1):} plateaus at 1{,}638 after
  step~$\approx$1{,}300. \textbf{Right (RL-2):} climbs to 4{,}836 but decelerates;
  unlike RL-1 it has not gone flat by step~200.}
  \label{fig:rl-coverage}
\end{figure}

The decisive caveat is scale. Run~2 ran $\approx$200 steps --- roughly $40\times$
shorter than run~1's 8{,}370 --- and its coverage trajectory, while decelerating,
had not gone flat the way run~1's did. That non-flatness is itself weak evidence:
the cumulative curve counts PCs from rejected programs (via the soft floor) over the
whole load-path trace, and by the measurement-noise model of
Section~\ref{sec:diversity-wall} a slowly-climbing, never-quite-flat curve is also
what background-PC accumulation alone produces, so it cannot be read as continued
genuine discovery. Two hundred steps is in any case too short to decide whether a
KCOV-reward loop can break past the SFT ceiling. RQ2 therefore remains \emph{open}:
run~2 establishes that the loop is healthy and localises the obstacle, but does not
close the question.

\section{The Diversity Wall}
\label{sec:diversity-wall}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

The central finding of this thesis concerns the metric that counts: unique KCOV PCs
from valid programs. It does not grow with the number of valid programs.

Within a single SFT-2 generation run at a \emph{fixed} 1024-token budget,
increasing the number of valid programs by roughly $16\times$ (from~$\approx$80
to~$\approx$1{,}300) raises the cumulative count of unique valid PCs by only about
$+28\%$ (replicate range $+9$ to $+45\%$; Figure~\ref{fig:saturation}). Holding the
budget fixed is what isolates the variable of interest: the near-flat coverage
reflects more programs, not longer ones, so it cannot be read as a side effect of a
budget change. Coverage grows far sub-linearly: the thousandth valid program adds
almost nothing the first hundred had not already reached. The model's valid programs
march the same verifier paths.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/saturation.pdf}
  \caption{Cumulative unique valid PCs versus number of valid programs. The band is
  the median and min--max over three replicates of a single SFT-2 run at a fixed
  1024-token budget; the 512-token endpoint and RL-2 are shown as separate points.
  Coverage rises steeply over the first $\approx$100 valid programs, then flattens.
  RL-2 (green point, mean $\pm$ replicate range) lands inside the band --- training
  did not push coverage past the SFT ceiling. The valid-unique-PC metric is
  nondeterministic ($\pm\sim$12\% at $n\approx1{,}300$), a noise quantified in the
  text; the sub-linear trend is robust across every replicate, the exact percentage
  is not.}
  \label{fig:saturation}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A caveat on the metric: the noise it commits.}
The unique-PC count carries a measurement noise that must be characterized before
any percentage is read off the curve, because the error is not the absolute level
but a random variable injected by kernel activity unrelated to the program. The
trace is taken over the whole \texttt{BPF\_PROG\_LOAD} path, not the verifier alone
(Section~\ref{sec:kcov-validator}), and reloading the \emph{same} accepted program
does not return the same PC set. Measured directly --- a fixed program reloaded a
few hundred times --- the trace decomposes into a \emph{deterministic core} of
$\approx$1{,}800--2{,}300 PCs present on every load, and a \emph{random component}
of $\approx$120$\pm$100 PCs per load (range $\approx$10--320), drawn from a
$\approx$450-PC pool of background-kernel addresses: allocation, soft-irq, and
scheduler paths that KCOV records during the load but that say nothing about the
program. The deterministic core cancels in any comparison and is not the error;
the random component is. It is independent of program depth --- a two-instruction
program and a 39-instruction one show the same $\approx$120$\pm$100 spread --- so it
is a property of the measurement channel, not of what the model generates. This
noise enters the cumulative-unique curve twice: it perturbs every individual point,
and because each load contributes a fresh draw from the pool, the cumulative union
keeps climbing --- by $\approx$200--450 PCs over a few hundred loads --- even for a
program that never changes. The $\pm\sim$12\% replicate spread at $n\approx1{,}300$
is exactly this random variable, and at that magnitude it is comparable to the
$+28\%$ separating the curve's two ends: no single percentage, and no
between-condition comparison at this effect size, is resolvable above it. What
\emph{does} survive the noise is the order of magnitude --- $16\times$ more programs
buy at most $\approx1.45\times$ the unique PCs across every replicate, nowhere near
the $16\times$ that linear growth would require. The claim is therefore made on the
\emph{sub-linear trend} alone, never on a single number or a condition-to-condition
difference.

\paragraph{Mode collapse, and where it originates.}
The coverage saturation (Figure~\ref{fig:saturation}) is the load-bearing evidence:
the model's valid programs march overlapping verifier paths, so unique coverage
grows far slower than program count. The narrow length distribution
(Figure~\ref{fig:depth}) is consistent with a collapsed generator but does not
establish it on its own --- two programs of equal length can walk entirely
different paths, so length variety is weaker evidence than path variety and is
reported as corroboration, not proof. Critically, both measurements are taken on
\emph{SFT-2's own generations}, so the saturation is a property of the supervised
base distribution; RL run~2 inherits it rather than creating it. This matches the
diversity- and entropy-collapse documented for GRPO under a scalar reward
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-rl-code}), and it reframes the open question: breaking the wall
may require widening the base distribution at the SFT stage, not only reshaping the
RL reward. Whether a longer, in-regime RL run or an explicitly diversity-preserving
objective can exceed the SFT ceiling is the central question left open (RQ2).

\paragraph{A preliminary contrastive cut: buzzer at an equal budget.}
A coverage race against buzzer --- the generator that produced the SFT corpus ---
gives a first, though confounded, external reference point
(Figure~\ref{fig:race}). At an equal submission budget of ${\sim}500$ programs,
buzzer's cumulative unique PCs ($4{,}915$) exceed the model's ($3{,}799$ over all
programs, $3{,}460$ from valid programs alone), and buzzer reaches that coverage with
far fewer accepted programs ($23$ against the model's $92$). A generator that is not
collapsed thus reaches more of the verifier at the same budget --- the pattern
expected of a model that has collapsed \emph{below} its teacher rather than one
faithfully imitating an already-narrow teacher. The comparison is not clean: buzzer
records coverage through its own in-process KCOV path while the model is scored
through the \texttt{kcov\_validator} oracle, and both were run once. Both paths
bracket KCOV around the \texttt{BPF\_PROG\_LOAD} call, so the quantities are
comparable in kind, but the differing collection setups and the single run make this
a confounded cut, not the controlled same-harness baseline. It is read as suggestive
evidence, not proof.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/coverage_race.pdf}
  \caption{Coverage race at an equal ${\sim}500$-program budget: cumulative unique
  kernel PCs for buzzer (logged through its own KCOV path) and SFT-2 (scored through
  \texttt{kcov\_validator}). Buzzer reaches $4{,}915$ PCs with $23$ valid programs;
  SFT-2 reaches $3{,}799$ over all programs ($3{,}460$ from valid programs) with $92$

  so the comparison is confounded --- suggestive of the model under-covering its
  teacher, not a controlled same-harness result.}
  \label{fig:race}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What this establishes, and what it does not.}
Cumulative coverage rises sub-linearly with sample count for \emph{every} fuzzing
corpus --- the diminishing-returns shape alone is not evidence of a defect. Two
features make the present saturation load-bearing rather than routine: it sets in
early (coverage flattens after $\approx$100 valid programs, and $16\times$ more
programs buy at most $1.45\times$ the unique PCs), and it does not move under
either intervention meant to break it --- scaling validity (RL run~2) or doubling
the token budget, neither of which widens the coverage reached. What the experiment
does \emph{not} yet contain is a \emph{clean} contrastive baseline: the buzzer race
above points the right way but is confounded by its separate coverage path and single
run, so a diverse generator measured on this \emph{exact} harness is still needed to
settle whether a non-collapsed generator escapes the same flattening. Nor is the absolute
level anchored --- the fraction of the verifier's reachable PCs that $\approx$3{,}685
represents is not measured, so saturation is not, on these data, distinguished from
near-exhaustion on absolute grounds; the claim rests on the \emph{relative}
flattening, not an absolute coverage level. Two further confounds bear on the
\emph{cause} of the narrowness, not just its magnitude. The SFT corpus is drawn
entirely from buzzer's two hand-written strategies
(Section~\ref{sec:buzzer-data}), so a narrow generation mode is equally consistent
with a generator that collapsed \emph{below} its teacher and with one that faithfully
imitates an already-narrow teacher; the buzzer race above is a first measurement of
the teacher's own path diversity and points to the former --- buzzer reaches more
coverage at equal budget --- but its harness confound keeps it from settling the
question. And the measurement pipeline itself filters toward uniformity before coverage
is read: the reward's 15-instruction floor, the encoder's silent dropping of
unparseable lines (Section~\ref{sec:encoder}), and generations that run to the token
cap together admit only programs of a certain length and syntax, so part of the
observed uniformity may be imposed by the harness rather than emitted by the
generator. Finally, the saturation curve rests on three replicates under a
$\pm\sim$12\% nondeterministic metric, so the band is an estimate, not a tight bound.
The conservative claim the data support is therefore:
\emph{within this model and this metric}, valid-program coverage saturates, the
saturation originates at the SFT stage, and neither RL nor longer programs move it.
A first, confounded cut at the contrastive baseline --- the buzzer race above ---
already points toward mode collapse; the \emph{clean} same-harness comparison that
would convert ``consistent with mode collapse'' into a settled finding remains future
work (Section~\ref{sec:future-work}).
