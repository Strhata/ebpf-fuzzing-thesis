\chapter{Experimental Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter presents the experiments in the order they were run, because each
run's failure motivated the next design change: SFT-1 produced valid but trivial
programs; RL run~1 starved its own gradient; a reward and format redesign followed;
SFT-2 produced deep programs but revealed a diversity weakness; RL run~2 cleared the
starvation but ran into that weakness as a wall. The chapter closes by
characterizing the wall directly.

A note on what counts. Per Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}, the metric of interest is
the number of \emph{unique KCOV PCs reached by valid programs}; verifier pass-rate
is a diagnostic, never a result. A model that emits a single trivial valid program
scores a near-perfect pass-rate and explores nothing. Percentages therefore appear
below only as diagnostics, and always with that caveat.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{SFT-1: Valid but Trivial}
\label{sec:results-sft}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:sft-training}

Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B~\cite{qwen25coder} was fine-tuned on the 27{,}514-example SFT
dataset using rank-16 QLoRA on the Q, K, V, and O projections of all attention
layers, for 3 epochs (9{,}288 gradient steps), effective batch size~8 via gradient
accumulation, maximum sequence length 768~tokens, learning rate
$2\times10^{-4}$ with AdamW 8-bit, BF16 with FlashAttention-2. The evaluation loss
fell from 0.637 to 0.557 and stabilised from the end of epoch~2 onward, with train
and eval loss tracking closely (no significant overfitting).

\subsection{What the Model Could Do}
\label{sec:sft1-capability}

The fine-tuned model learned BPF syntax and register conventions: it generates
programs that the verifier accepts, where the zero-shot base model produces almost
none. But the programs are \emph{trivial} --- typically one or two instructions.
The cause is the representation: the verifier-log assembly format
(Section~\ref{sec:format-decision}) costs roughly 232~tokens per instruction once
the register-state annotations are included, so the completion budget leaves room
for only a handful of instructions. The model could produce valid BPF, but not
programs long enough to exercise anything interesting in the verifier.

As a diagnostic only: measured through the pure-Python encoder, SFT-1's syntactic
accept rate is about 73\% (the frequently-quoted ``60\%'' was an artifact of a
clang parser that mis-reads verifier register-state lines as instructions). This
number is not a result. A 73\% accept rate on
two-instruction programs discovers nothing, and is recorded only to make the point
that \emph{validity is not coverage}. SFT-1 answers RQ1 in the narrow,
prerequisite sense of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}: the model acquired BPF syntax,
not the ability to explore.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{RL Run 1: Gradient Starvation}
\label{sec:results-rl}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

RL run~1 (\texttt{rl\_grpo\_v2}) used GRPO with $G=4$, $\beta=0.01$, maximum
completion length 600~tokens, on the local RTX~4070 Laptop GPU, for 8{,}370 steps.
It did not learn. Cumulative new-PC growth ceased at approximately step~1{,}300 and
nothing further was discovered in the remaining $\approx$7{,}000 steps
(Figure~\ref{fig:rl-coverage}, left).

\subsection{Coverage}
\label{sec:coverage}

Two distinct quantities must be separated. The \emph{total} coverage frontier at
the end of the run was 1{,}638 distinct PCs, but this includes the
$\approx$1{,}501 PCs already reached during SFT evaluation; the \emph{incrementally
new} contribution of RL was only 137 PCs
(\texttt{results/rl\_analysis\_pcs.csv}; the README's 138 is an off-by-one
counting artifact). Growth was bursty and brief: no new PCs for the first 97
batches, then a series of bursts up to batch~1{,}241, after which only two further
PCs were ever found (batches 3{,}036 and 4{,}873).

\subsection{Diagnosis: \texttt{reward\_std} Collapse}
\label{sec:plateau-results}

The cause is visible directly in the reward variance
(Figure~\ref{fig:rl-std}, left). Across the run, the within-group reward standard
deviation $\sigma_r$ was zero in 99.5\% of batches (mean $\sigma_r \approx 0.001$).
The GRPO advantage is $A_i=(r_i-\bar r)/\sigma_r$
(Equation~\ref{eq:grpo-advantage}); when $\sigma_r=0$ the gradient is identically
zero and no update occurs --- the gradient-starvation failure mode
of~\cite{grpo-starvation}. The few non-zero-variance batches coincide almost
exactly with the discovery events: a batch that finds a new PC briefly creates
reward spread, then variance returns to zero.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/rl_reward_std.pdf}
  \caption{Within-group reward standard deviation per training step. \textbf{Left
  (RL-1):} $\sigma_r$ is exactly zero in 99.5\% of batches (mean $0.001$) --- the
  gradient starves. \textbf{Right (RL-2 phase~B):} the validity-gated soft-floor
  reward keeps $\sigma_r$ alive (mean $0.224$, range $0.01$--$0.44$), never
  sustained-zero. Source: WandB runs \texttt{hapj9sah}, \texttt{bz5ymfzl}.}
  \label{fig:rl-std}
\end{figure}

The structural cause was twofold. First, a bug in \texttt{kcov\_validator} returned
an empty PC list for every REJECTED program, so all rejected completions looked
identical to the reward function (fixed in commit~\texttt{c8a9d02}). Second, SFT-1
was prompted with a \texttt{Status:~VALID} header, and during RL inference it
produced accepted programs 99.7\% of the time; once the coverage frontier
saturated, every completion in a group received the same reward. The KCOV bug is
the primary and sufficient cause of the zero variance; the prompt bias is a
secondary contributor. The \emph{result} of run~1 is this diagnosis --- not the 137
PCs it happened to touch.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Reward and Format Redesign}
\label{sec:reward-validation}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two changes followed from the run-1 diagnosis. The format was stripped of its
verbose register-state header, so that real programs --- not just one or two
instructions --- fit the completion budget. And the reward was redesigned to
guarantee within-group variance (Section~\ref{sec:reward-redesign}): first a
verdict-blind depth reward, which was rejected because it optimizes coverage from
programs the verifier never accepts, and then the validity-gated soft-floor reward
(Equation~\ref{eq:reward-v2}) carried into run~2.

A 100-step comparison run validated the two structural fixes. Removing the
\texttt{Status:~VALID} header shifted the verdict distribution from 99.7\% accepted
(run~1) to 11.5\% accepted / 63.2\% rejected / 25.2\% encode-fail, confirming that
the prompt had anchored generation toward valid programs. Crucially, those rejected
programs now returned \emph{non-empty} KCOV traces from the fixed binary, so the
reward function could assign them differentiated depth scores --- the precondition
for non-zero $\sigma_r$ in a predominantly-rejected regime.

\section{SFT-2: Deep Programs, Narrow Distribution}
\label{sec:results-sft2}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

SFT-2 (\texttt{sft-1epoch-v2}) retrains on the stripped format with
\texttt{[coverage]}/\texttt{[novelty]} control tokens, for one epoch
(2{,}408~steps). The capability gain over SFT-1 is real: the model now generates
\emph{deep} programs --- a median of 30 instructions at a 512-token budget, against
SFT-1's one or two (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}). These are programs long enough to
exercise the verifier's analysis.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/depth_collapse.pdf}
  \caption{SFT-2 program-length distribution (canonical bytecode count). The
  distribution is narrow and set by the token budget: mean $30.2\pm3.5$
  instructions at 512~tokens, $57.6\pm8.6$ at 1024 (CV~$\approx0.12$--$0.15$). The
  median simply scales with the budget; the model does not vary program length ---
  the first sign of mode collapse.}
  \label{fig:depth}
\end{figure}

The same figure exposes the weakness. The length distribution is \emph{narrow}
(coefficient of variation $\approx 0.12$ at 512~tokens) and is pinned by the token
budget: doubling the budget shifts the median from 30 to 58 instructions but does
not widen the spread. The model emits essentially one characteristic program length
rather than a diverse range. SFT-2's inference accept rate is low ($\approx 7.5\%$,
a diagnostic, not a result): deep programs have far more surface on which to fail
the verifier than two-instruction ones, so depth and validity trade off. This is
the model that the diversity experiment and RL run~2 are built on.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{RL Run 2: Starvation Cleared, Ceiling Intact}
\label{sec:run2}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

RL run~2 trains SFT-2 with the validity-gated soft-floor reward
(Equation~\ref{eq:reward-v2}), $G=16$, in two phases: a 20-step smoke test
(phase~A) and a 200-step run with global novelty enabled (phase~B,
\texttt{bz5ymfzl}).

The mechanism result is robust. The reward variance stays alive throughout:
$\sigma_r$ has mean $0.224$ (range $0.01$--$0.44$) over all 207 steps, against
run~1's mean $0.001$ with 99\% of batches at exactly zero
(Figure~\ref{fig:rl-std}). The soft floor structurally guarantees within-group
variance even at the $\approx7\%$ accept rate, so the gradient starvation that
halted run~1 \emph{does not recur}. This is a property of the reward design, not a
trend that could reverse.

The coverage result is negative but inconclusive. Cumulative PCs climbed during
training (4{,}320~$\to$~4{,}836 total PCs, including those from rejected programs
via the soft floor) but with a clearly decelerating rate
(Figure~\ref{fig:rl-coverage}, right). On the metric that counts --- unique PCs
from \emph{valid} programs --- run~2's checkpoint scores $\approx$3{,}685 (replicate
range 3{,}606--3{,}833), squarely within the SFT-2 band
(Section~\ref{sec:diversity-wall}): RL did not push coverage beyond the SFT
ceiling. Validity nudged upward (from $\approx7.5\%$ to $\approx8.7\%$), consistent
with the gate but a single under-powered measurement.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/rl_coverage.pdf}
  \caption{Cumulative unique PCs per training step (total, including
  rejected-program PCs). \textbf{Left (RL-1):} plateaus at 1{,}638 after
  step~$\approx$1{,}300. \textbf{Right (RL-2):} climbs to 4{,}836 but decelerates;
  unlike RL-1 it has not gone flat by step~200.}
  \label{fig:rl-coverage}
\end{figure}

The decisive caveat is scale. Run~2 ran $\approx$200 steps --- roughly $40\times$
shorter than run~1's 8{,}370 --- and its coverage trajectory, while decelerating,
had not gone flat the way run~1's did. Two hundred steps is too short to decide
whether a KCOV-reward loop can break past the SFT ceiling. RQ2 therefore remains
\emph{open}: run~2 establishes that the loop is healthy and localises the obstacle,
but does not close the question.

\section{The Diversity Wall}
\label{sec:diversity-wall}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

The central finding of this thesis concerns the metric that counts: unique KCOV PCs
from valid programs. It does not grow with the number of valid programs.

Within a single SFT-2 generation run, increasing the number of valid programs by
roughly $16\times$ (from~$\approx$80 to~$\approx$1{,}300) raises the cumulative
count of unique valid PCs by only about $+28\%$
(Figure~\ref{fig:saturation}). Coverage grows far sub-linearly: the
thousandth valid program adds almost nothing the first hundred had not already
reached. The model's valid programs march the same verifier paths.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/saturation.pdf}
  \caption{Cumulative unique valid PCs versus number of valid programs (SFT-2,
  three replicates shown as a median line with a min--max band). Coverage rises
  steeply over the first $\approx$100 valid programs, then flattens. RL-2 (green
  point, mean $\pm$ replicate range) lands inside the band --- training did not push
  coverage past the SFT ceiling. The valid-unique-PC metric is nondeterministic
  ($\pm\sim$12\% at $n\approx1{,}300$); the sub-linear trend is robust across every
  replicate, the exact percentage is not.}
  \label{fig:saturation}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A caveat on the metric.}
Re-validating the \emph{same} programs gives different unique-PC counts run to run.
KCOV records every kernel PC touched during the trace, including background
interrupt and scheduler activity that accumulates with program count, and a few
borderline verdicts flip on timing. The measured spread is $\pm\sim$1\% at 75 valid
programs but $\pm\sim$12\% at 1{,}300. For this reason the result is reported as a
range over replicates and the claim is made on the \emph{sub-linear trend}, which
holds across every replicate (even the most generous: $16\times$ more programs buy
at most $1.45\times$ the coverage), never on a single percentage.

\paragraph{Mode collapse, and where it originates.}
The narrow length distribution (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}) and the coverage saturation
(Figure~\ref{fig:saturation}) are two faces of one phenomenon: the generator has
collapsed onto a narrow mode of similar programs that traverse overlapping verifier
paths. Neither scaling validity (RL run~2) nor doubling program length
(512~$\to$~1024 tokens) escapes it. Critically, both measurements are taken on
\emph{SFT-2's own generations}, so the wall is a property of the supervised base
distribution; RL run~2 inherits it rather than creating it. This matches the
diversity- and entropy-collapse documented for GRPO under a scalar reward
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-rl-code}), and it reframes the open question: breaking the wall may
require widening the base distribution at the SFT stage, not only reshaping the RL
reward. Whether a longer, in-regime RL run or an explicitly diversity-preserving
objective can exceed the SFT ceiling is the central question left open (RQ2).
