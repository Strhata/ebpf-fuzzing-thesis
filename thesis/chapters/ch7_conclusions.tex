\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

This thesis asked whether a language model, fine-tuned on real BPF
program--verifier-log pairs and trained with reinforcement learning against live
kernel coverage, could drive systematic exploration of the Linux eBPF verifier. To
the best of our knowledge no prior work pursues this specific direction --- an
LLM-generated, coverage-rewarded fuzzer for the BPF verifier --- and building it
yields three contributions. The honest headline is that the system works
end-to-end and the training loop learns, but the generator does not yet explore
diversely: \emph{diversity, not validity, is the wall.}

\textbf{An end-to-end LLM-driven fuzzing system.}
A complete platform was designed and implemented: an instrumented buzzer dumps the
kernel FFI boundary as JSONL to build the corpus; a QLoRA stage fine-tunes the
generator; a GRPO stage rewards it with live KCOV coverage; and a Go binary inside
a KCOV-instrumented Linux~6.8 VM validates each program and returns its PC trace.
The central engineering obstacle --- bridging LLM text to the raw 8-byte BPF
instruction structs the kernel requires --- is solved by a pure-Python encoder that
reads the opcode byte directly from the verifier-log format and needs no clang at
inference time. The scaffolding is modular: retargeting to another kernel
subsystem requires only new training data and a new reward.

\textbf{The diversity wall: a characterized negative result.}
The metric that matters is the number of unique KCOV PCs reached by \emph{valid}
programs. Supervised fine-tuning solved validity --- the model progressed from
trivial one- and two-instruction programs to deep, ${\sim}30$-instruction ones ---
but validity did not buy exploration. Coverage \emph{saturates}: increasing the
number of valid programs by ${\sim}16\times$ raises unique valid PCs by only
${\sim}28\%$ (replicate range $+9$ to $+45\%$), and the generator collapses onto a
narrow mode --- the same verifier paths walked repeatedly. Neither scaling validity
nor doubling program length escapes it.
Because these measurements are taken on the supervised model's own generations, the
collapse \emph{originates at the SFT base distribution}; the RL stage inherits it.
Sub-linear coverage growth is the universal shape of a fuzzing corpus; what makes it
load-bearing here is that it sets in early, resists both interventions meant to
break it, and originates upstream of the RL stage. The experiment lacks a
contrastive baseline --- a diverse generator, or buzzer at equal budget on this
harness --- that would separate this both from ordinary diminishing returns and from
faithful imitation of buzzer's narrow two-strategy corpus, and so convert the
characterization into a proof; that comparison is the natural next experiment
(Section~\ref{sec:future-work}). A characterized negative result with an identified
mechanism is the central empirical contribution of this thesis.

\textbf{GRPO gradient starvation: diagnosis and a structural fix.}
RL run~1 stalled completely: the within-group reward standard deviation $\sigma_r$
was zero in 99.5\% of batches, zeroing the GRPO gradient. The cause was a
homogeneous reward: a \texttt{Status:~VALID} prompt anchored generation so that
99.6\% of completions were accepted, and once the coverage frontier saturated every
accepted program scored the same flat value, collapsing the within-group spread. A
latent \texttt{kcov\_validator} bug that returned empty traces for rejected programs
would have flattened the reward too, but at run~1's 0.3\% rejection rate it was
immaterial; it mattered only once the prompt header was removed and rejection became
common. The fix is a two-part reward redesign: a per-program novelty term that
separates otherwise-equal accepted programs, and a validity-gated soft floor that
grants rejected programs graded partial credit for how far the verifier walked ---
together holding within-group variance away from zero across the group compositions
the policy produces in practice, while an accepted program that clears the length
floor always scores strictly higher so the policy is still pushed toward validity.
The argument is structural, not the empirical run-to-run comparison: run~2 changed
several hyperparameters at once
($G$, prompt format, SFT base, $\beta$), so its held-alive variance ($\sigma_r$ mean
$0.224$ against run~1's $0.001$, starvation absent) is consistent with the proof
rather than independent confirmation of it. That a coverage reward must guarantee
within-group variance to be trainable under GRPO is a reusable finding, independent
of the eBPF setting.

\medskip
Against the research questions of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}: \textbf{RQ1} is
answered, but only as a prerequisite --- the model learns to emit valid BPF, yet
validity alone explores nothing. \textbf{RQ2} --- whether a KCOV-reward loop can
push coverage beyond the SFT ceiling --- remains \emph{open}: run~2 cleared the
starvation but ran only $\approx$200 steps, roughly $40\times$ shorter than run~1,
and the cumulative curve that had not yet flattened is consistent with load-path
measurement noise rather than necessarily with new discovery
(Chapter~\ref{ch:results}), so the question is unresolved rather than answered
negatively.
\textbf{RQ3} is answered in full: two failure modes are characterized --- gradient
starvation, diagnosed and fixed, and the diversity wall, characterized and left
open. The most actionable conclusion is that, because the collapse originates at
the SFT stage, the next effort should widen the \emph{base} distribution, not only
reshape the RL reward.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

The open problem is sharp and well-localised: break the diversity wall. The
directions below are ordered from the immediate confound-settling experiments this
thesis directly motivates to the deeper changes its central finding points to.

\begin{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Finish the RL-2 experiment in-regime.}
        Run~2 was ${\sim}40\times$ shorter than run~1 and was benchmarked outside
        its training regime. A longer run, evaluated at the training temperature
        and length, is needed before any conclusion about RQ2. The remote-reward
        pipeline built for run~2 is already in place to carry it.

  \item \textbf{A controlled length sweep.}
        Generate at several token budgets and measure validity and unique valid PCs
        at each, to settle whether the depth--validity trade-off observed between
        512 and 1024 tokens is a genuine coupling or an artifact of confounded
        runs.

  \item \textbf{Diversity-preserving objectives.}
        Because a single scalar reward is known to induce the very mode collapse
        observed here (Section~\ref{sec:rw-rl-code}), the principled attack is an
        objective that rewards behavioural diversity directly: a
        quality-diversity / MAP-Elites archive keyed on verifier-path signatures,
        or a diversity-aware GRPO variant, together with a stronger persistent
        anti-revisit (novelty) penalty.

  \item \textbf{Widen the SFT base distribution.}
        Since the collapse originates at the supervised stage, the generator itself
        must be made more varied: stratifying or re-weighting the corpus by program
        structure, or a curriculum that explicitly rewards structural variety,
        rather than relying on the RL reward alone to introduce diversity a
        collapsed base cannot express.

  \item \textbf{Constrained decoding.}
        Enforce eBPF instruction semantics at generation time (e.g.\ a
        finite-state constraint on the token sequence) to raise the density of
        reward-eligible programs per batch and let RL spend its budget on
        exploration rather than on relearning syntax.

\end{enumerate}

A direct buzzer-versus-LLM coverage comparison at equal budget is a natural
evaluation once the wall is broken; until the generator explores diversely, such a
comparison would only restate the present finding, and is therefore deferred rather
than presented as a result.
